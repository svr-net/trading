How it works

    
The model. Every evening, after each market's close, one model per market (the UK, Europe, the US, Asia-Pacific and INDEX, UCITS index ETFs on Xetra) ranks its most traded shares from their daily prices and volumes. It looks for shares that fell on their own rather than with the market (residual reversal), and weights them more when the market is turbulent (Nagel's liquidity premium). It is fitted on the return over the 5 days a position can be held, and refitted every month on the years before only. Its bets shrink when its own results turn volatile (down to a quarter), so a bad run is ridden small rather than whole. A day with no bar for a share is filled with its last close for the model's inputs only: it never buys or sells on one.

    
The orders. Each pick is bought in the next opening auction only, never later in the day, at most at its own price cap: learnt from how the share usually opens and what opening that far above the close has cost or added before, so a strong pick buys more surely and a weak one avoids paying up. It is offered at each close at or above its exit level: the first close that reaches it sells it, and otherwise it is sold on its fifth day. Every morning the day's model updates the positions held: one it would no longer buy is sold at the open, and the others get their exit level set again. The bridge on your NAS places all of this at Interactive Brokers (a paper account for now) and records the fills; Results compares them with what the model expected.

    
Costs and money. No cost enters the learning, the backtests or the split of the money; Interactive Brokers' commissions, UK stamp duty (none on AIM shares) and the European taxes come off only the next day's final order list: each order's expected return after its own costs, and an order expected to lose after them is not made. The account's daily limit is split over the markets half by their backtested records over the last 20 sessions, the period that matters for a 5-day hold (before costs, as the model learns; the most recent days counting most, a day 10 sessions old half as much; the model itself still learns from years) and half by their outlook: the next day's orders' expected return after their costs, times their chance to fill in the opening auction, cut to the share of its expectations the model actually delivered in the backtest. The split gives the most to the best risk-adjusted, at most half to one market, and on paper at least 5% each. Each market's earlier models run beside it as shadows, so a change is measured before it is kept. Prices are not published under the data licence.
