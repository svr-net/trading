Lab: the strategies on this device

    
The same strategy engine as the evening scan, compiled to WebAssembly and run here, in a background thread, on this market's daily bars from your Interactive Brokers account (through your bridge; the prices stay on this device). Choose a strategy and run it: its backtest (before costs) and the orders it would place next (costs applied at the end). IBKR's bars go back about two years and a strategy needs one of them to start, so the backtest is shorter than the scan's, which uses up to nine.

    

    
 

    

    

    

  


  

    
How it works

    
The strategies. Every evening, after each market's close, the strategy engine runs the market's strategy on its most traded shares' daily bars. The strategies are daily, from the research on short-horizon returns: RSI(2) pullbacks in an uptrend (Connors and Alvarez 2009), internal bar strength (closes near the day's low; Pagonidis 2013), daily residual reversal (Lehmann 1990; Nagel 2012), volume reversal (Conrad, Hameed and Niden 1994), the high-volume return premium (Gervais, Kaniel and Mingelgrin 2001), the turn of the month (McConnell and Xu 2008) and the 20-day breakout. Each is its own file in the engine and is chosen per market; the shares, prices and positions are the same for all.

    
The book. A market's book holds a few positions of equal size. After every close the strategy scores the shares and the highest scores are the ones to hold. A share it no longer wants is sold at the next open; one it wants and does not hold is bought in the next opening auction, at most a little above the last close, and tried again the next morning if the open was higher. Every position has a Parabolic SAR stop at IBKR (Wilder 1978): it starts under the lows before the buy and rises each day with the price; a share stopped out is bought back once its SAR turns up again, while the strategy still wants it. The bridge on your NAS places this at Interactive Brokers (a paper account for now) and records the fills; Results compares them with the backtest.

    
Costs and money. No cost enters the strategies, the backtests or the split of the money: they are judged on what the market did. Interactive Brokers' commissions, UK stamp duty (the UK trades AIM shares, which pay none) and the European taxes are applied only at the end, to the next day's order list: each order's expected return after its own costs, and one expected to lose after them is not placed. The orders are sized to their costs: a market's money goes over fewer, larger trades when the broker's minimums would eat a small one. The account's limit is split over the markets by their backtested records and their outlook, at most half to one market, and on paper at least 15% each. Prices are not published under the data licence.

    
How far to trust a backtest. The shares come from the data store, which holds shares listed today: most that collapsed or were delisted since 2018 are missing, so a share strategy's backtest overstates. Each scan therefore also runs the strategy's opposite picks on the same shares and book. The gap between the two, shown as the strategy's own edge, is the part to expect; what the opposite picks also make comes from the list. The universe is each day's most traded shares as of that day, and a bar with an impossible low or high is dropped, its last real close rolled over it.
